## Wellbeing Digest — 2026-06-14

Daily digest of the latest research on telomere biology, longevity pathways, epigenetic aging, and healthspan from Nature journals.

### 🔬 Nature — Telomere Research

**Structures of human telomerase with BIBR1532 reveal novel mechanism of inhibition**
*Nature Chemical Biology · June 2, 2026*

Researchers resolved nine cryo-EM structures of human telomerase bound to BIBR1532, a selective small-molecule inhibitor. BIBR1532 occupies a previously unknown pocket between the TERT finger and palm domains, disproportionately inhibiting the rate-limiting first step of telomere repeat addition. The rigid finger architecture revealed here explains telomerase's slow catalytic rate and low fidelity — findings directly enabling structure-based drug design for the ~85–90% of cancers that reactivate telomerase.
[Read article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41589-026-02238-6)

**Epigenetic control of telomeric RNA maintains heterochromatin in telomerase-driven cancers**
*Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy · June 4, 2026*

Using genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 and shRNA screens (synthetic dosage lethality approach), researchers identified 100 high-confidence genes required specifically in hTERT-overexpressing cancer cells. Top hit FTSJ3 — an RNA 2′-O-methyltransferase — maintains epigenetic silencing of telomeric RNA (TERRA); its depletion destabilises telomeric heterochromatin selectively in cancer cells. This reveals an exploitable vulnerability beyond direct hTERT inhibition, relevant to ~90% of telomerase-positive tumours.
[Read article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41392-026-02750-0)

**Centromeric footprints preserve telomere integrity in ALT cancers**
*Nature · June 3, 2026*

In the 5–10% of cancers using Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres (ALT), researchers discovered pathological insertions of centromeric α-satellite repeats and CENP-B boxes at telomeric locations in ALT cell lines and primary paediatric neuroblastomas. DiMeLo-seq revealed discrete CENP-A chromatin footprints at telomeric sites. ATRX loss and DNA hypomethylation drive ALT activation and facilitate acquisition of these centromeric marks, which paradoxically stabilise telomere integrity — a new epigenetic mechanism sustaining genomic stability in telomerase-negative tumours.
[Read article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10598-1)

### 🧪 Nature — Longevity & Aging

**A damage accumulation model identifies distinct aging regimes across species**
*Nature Aging · June 9, 2026*

Applying a stochastic saturating removal model to survival data spanning seven orders of magnitude in lifespan, researchers identified two distinct aging regimes: 'ballistic aging' (damage production outpaces removal) and a diffusive regime (stochastic fluctuations dominate). Key parameters — removal rate ratio, noise amplitude, death threshold — showed near-universal values across species, while the damage production rate (spanning 7 orders of magnitude) best predicts lifespan. Provides a unified mathematical basis for translating longevity findings from model organisms to humans.
[Read article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-026-01138-7)

**Corylin promotes healthy aging via RAGA–mTOR suppression and sex-dependent activation of SIRT3**
*Nature Communications · June 10, 2026*

Mid-life Corylin (a Psoralea corylifolia flavonoid) extended median lifespan by 11.9% in female mice with 33% higher survival at 125 weeks, with no comparable benefit in males. Multi-omics analyses showed Corylin suppresses mTOR signalling via direct RAGA GTPase interaction and sex-dependently activates mitochondrial SIRT3. The sex-specific effect highlights hormonal context as a critical variable in longevity pharmacology, positioning Corylin alongside rapamycin as a promising mTOR-targeted anti-aging compound.
[Read article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74184-9)

**A progeria syndrome links DNA hypermethylation to age-related pathology**
*Nature Genetics · June 12, 2026*

Heyn–Sproul–Jackson syndrome (DNMT3A gain-of-function mutations) recapitulates age-related gains in DNA methylation, causes multilineage stem cell dysfunction, and phenocopies aging in humans and mice — establishing that DNA hypermethylation at lineage-specific loci is a causal driver of age-related tissue failure, not merely a correlate. Region-specific CpG hypermethylation suppressed stem cell output and lineage commitment, mirroring normal aging declines. Provides the first genetic proof that epigenetic drift causes aging pathology, with implications for DNMT3A-targeted reversal strategies.
[Read article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-026-02633-8)

**Epigenetic control of telomeric RNA maintains heterochromatin in telomerase-driven cancers**

ATRX loss combined with DNA hypomethylation facilitates this epigenetic rearrangement; interfering with HJURP-mediated CENP-A deposition compromises telomere integrity in ALT cells. *20 May 2026 · Nature Structural & Molecular Biology*

The Rap1 protein is a core component of the shelterin-like telomere protection complex. This study demonstrates that dense Rap1 coverage physically blocks access of the Mre11–Rad50–Xrs2 (MRX/MRN) DNA damage sensing complex to telomere ends, preventing inappropriate activation of the DNA damage response at chromosome termini. Loss of Rap1 binding exposes telomeric double-strand breaks to MRX recognition, triggering checkpoint signaling. This structural insight refines our understanding of how cells distinguish natural chromosome ends from broken DNA — a critical distinction for genome stability and cellular senescence. *(Abstract only)*

### Nature — Longevity & Aging

**Mechanism of age-related accumulation of mtDNA mutations in human blood**
*27 May 2026 · Nature*

Analyzing whole-genome sequences from ~750,000 individuals, this landmark study finds that mitochondrial DNA single-nucleotide variants (mtSNVs) accumulate sharply at age 60, occurring at low heteroplasmy levels with little evidence of positive selection. Contrary to the prevailing oxidative damage hypothesis, the mutational spectrum is more consistent with mtDNA replication errors. A GWAS for heteroplasmic mtSNV burden identified germline variants near TERT, TCL1A, and SMC4 — all linked to clonal haematopoiesis — establishing CH as the primary driver of age-associated mtDNA mutation accumulation. *(Open Access)*

*22 May 2026 · Nature Communications*

AmiAge is a Random Forest biological age clock trained on plasma concentrations of 18 amino acids from >11,000 in-house samples spanning ages 1–89, validated against >270,000 publicly available samples. The AmiAge Gap (predicted minus chronological age) strongly correlates with telomere attrition, frailty indices, and age-related disease incidence. A distilled 8-amino acid model (alanine, glutamine, glycine, histidine, leucine, phenylalanine, tyrosine, valine) retains full predictive power. The clock's simplicity and scalability make it suitable for routine clinical use. *(Open Access)*

Tony Wyss-Coray (Stanford) and Eric Topol (Scripps) critically appraise the biological-clock field — epigenetic (Horvath, GrimAge, DunedinPACE), proteomic/organ-specific, transcriptomic, immune, and imaging/AI-based clocks. They frame clocks against interventions aimed at slowing or reversing aging — epigenetic reprogramming, thymic rejuvenation, senolytics, plasma exchange — arguing the clocks' real value is as **readouts of whether an intervention did anything**. Three use cases: risk stratification, prevention/early detection, and endpoint measurement (citing COSMOS, DO-HEALTH, and semaglutide slowing epigenetic aging). Their caution: clock outputs remain poorly calibrated, inconsistently validated across competing clocks, and vulnerable to conflating damage with adaptation. *Abstract only — subscription content.*

**Time-restricted feeding extends healthspan in both sexes and lifespan in male C57BL/6 J mice**
*Nature Aging · 02 Jun 2026 · Open Access*

The Takahashi lab tested lifelong circadian-aligned time-restricted feeding (**TRF**) on regular chow in **264 male and 264 female mice**, comparing 12-h and 8-h nightly windows against ad libitum controls. TRF improved behavioral rhythmicity, body weight/composition and a 31-item frailty index; a composite Healthspan Index confirmed extended healthspan in both sexes, proportionally greater in females. The narrower 8-h window drove self-imposed caloric restriction (**intake ↓ ~9–23%**) and was the only regimen to extend lifespan, raising **male median lifespan ~12% (818 → 916 days)** while females showed no significant extension. Notably, benefits occurred **without sustained changes in fasting glucose, insulin or inflammatory markers**, implying TRF acts largely through enhanced circadian organization and temporal control of substrate use rather than systemic endocrine shifts.
