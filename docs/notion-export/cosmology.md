### Scientific American — Cosmology

#### Did we just see a primordial black hole at the Milky Way's edge?

**Date:** June 4, 2026
**URL:** https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/did-we-just-see-a-primordial-black-hole-at-the-milky-ways-edge/

Researchers led by Renee Key (Swinburne University) report a candidate primordial black hole (PBH) dubbed 'Phoebe' detected via gravitational microlensing of a star in the Large Magellanic Cloud. The object has a best-fit mass of ~3 lunar masses, located ~60,000 light-years away in the Milky Way's dark-matter-rich halo, moving at ~300 km/s. PBHs form from density perturbations in the first ~0.0001 s after the Big Bang; if they constitute all dark matter, their mass must lie in the asteroid-to-lunar range (10^17 to 10^23 g). The microlensing signal lasted ~1 hour, consistent with a sub-stellar compact object; stellar variability and free-floating planet interpretations were statistically disfavored. Independent support comes from 12 candidate lunar-mass microlensing events toward Andromeda from the Subaru Telescope. If confirmed, Phoebe would be the first direct observational evidence of PBHs as dark matter candidates.

#### Newly discovered ripples in spacetime put Einstein's general relativity to the test

**Date:** March 5, 2026
**URL:** https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/newly-discovered-ripples-in-spacetime-put-einsteins-general-relativity-to/

The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration has released its fourth observing run catalog, more than doubling the total number of gravitational-wave (GW) detections. The expanded catalog includes binary black hole (BBH), black hole-neutron star (BHNS), and binary neutron star (BNS) events, with newly observed systems featuring high spins and asymmetric mass ratios. Each detection serves as a precision test of General Relativity in the strong-field regime; so far no statistically significant deviations from GR have been found, but constraints on post-Newtonian parameters are tightening with each new event. The catalog is published in the Astrophysical Journal Letters.

**Date:** May 15, 2026 | [Read Article](https://www.quantamagazine.org/rubin-tracks-skyscraper-size-asteroids-failed-supernovas-and-interstellar-visitors-20260515/)

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST) at Cerro Pachón has begun its 10-year Legacy Survey of Space and Time, imaging the entire southern sky every few nights with a 3.2-billion-pixel camera to unprecedented depth (100× fainter than previous sky surveys). Early results include 19 rapidly rotating asteroids, one completing a revolution in `T = 1.88 min` — far below the spin-barrier `ω_max = √(4πGρ/3)` expected for rubble-pile bodies, implying monolithic structure. The photometric redshift pipeline will measure `z_phot` for ~4 billion galaxies, enabling precision dark energy constraints from the BAO scale `r_s ≈ 147 Mpc` and Type Ia supernova distance ladder to address the Hubble tension (`H₀^local − H₀^CMB ≈ 5σ`). Rubin also detected the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS 10 days before its official announcement, demonstrating its transient-detection pipeline's sensitivity.

### Quanta Magazine — Black Holes

#### Entanglement Builds Space-Time. Now "Magic" Gives It Gravity.

**Date:** June 3, 2026 | [Read Article](https://www.quantamagazine.org/entanglement-builds-space-time-now-magic-gives-it-gravity-20260603/)

Charles Cao (Virginia Tech), John Preskill (Caltech), and collaborators have identified "magic" — measured by the count of non-Clifford (T) gates required to prepare a quantum state — as the quantum ingredient that gives holographic space-time its gravitational flexibility. In the AdS/CFT framework, prior stabilizer codes (zero magic) produced inert geometries where the metric perturbation `δg_μν` decoupled from matter. Adding magic via non-Clifford gates enables `δg_μν ≠ 0` in response to stress-energy `T_μν` — precisely Einstein's equations `G_μν = 8πG T_μν`. Gravity therefore emerges from *approximate* (rather than perfect) quantum error correction: the imperfection of the code is what makes Newton's apple fall.

#### In Expanding de Sitter Space, Quantum Mechanics Gets Even More Elusive

**Date:** March 30, 2026 | [Read Article](https://www.quantamagazine.org/in-expanding-de-sitter-space-quantum-mechanics-gets-even-more-elusive-20260330/)

Shalma Wegsman reviews the deep tension between quantum mechanics and de Sitter space — geometry with positive cosmological constant `Λ > 0`, metric `ds² = −dt² + e^(2Ht) d𝐱²` with `H = √(Λ/3)` — that most closely describes our universe. Unlike AdS, de Sitter's cosmological horizon at `χ_H = 1/H` cuts off any accessible boundary, leaving no gauge-invariant notion of particle or S-matrix. Penedones and Loparco (EPFL) showed massless photons in de Sitter can decompose into massive particles, implying spontaneous decay `γ → m + m̄` allowed because energy is not conserved under cosmic expansion. Physicists are importing holographic black hole techniques to make sense of quantum gravity in de Sitter, but the absence of a single shared horizon for all observers remains the central obstruction.
