## PAGE 1 — 🌐 Key Sources & Web Resources — Fine-Structure Constant α
*(Compiled 9 Jul 2026)*

### The accepted value (CODATA 2022)
- **α⁻¹ = 137.035999177(21)** — i.e. α ≈ 1/137.035999177 ≈ **0.0072973525643**
- Relative standard uncertainty: **1.6 × 10⁻¹⁰** (based on all data through 31 Dec 2022)
- Sources: NIST CODATA 2022 wallet card (physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/wallet_2022.pdf) · codata.org · NIST publication (CODATA 2022)

### Landmark precision measurements
- **LKB Paris (2020)** — rubidium-87 recoil, matter-wave interferometry (Morel, Yao, Cladé, Guellati-Khélifa): **α⁻¹ = 137.035999206(11)**, accuracy **81 parts per trillion** — the most precise to date. Nature (2020): nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2964-7
- **UC Berkeley (2018)** — cesium-133 recoil, Müller group (Parker et al.), "Measurement of the fine-structure constant as a test of the Standard Model": **α = 1/137.035999046(27)**, 0.2 ppb. Science (2018): science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aap7706
- ⚠️ The Rb (LKB) and Cs (Berkeley) results **disagree in the tenth digit** — a >5σ tension that is still unresolved and could hint at new physics.
- Explainers: Quanta Magazine "Physicists Nail Down the Magic Number" (2020) · Scientific American "Physicists Achieve Best-Ever Measurement of Fine-Structure Constant"

### Electron g−2 / QED test
- The electron anomalous magnetic moment g−2 gives an independent route to α. QED prediction agrees with experiment to **>10 significant figures** — among the most precisely verified predictions in physics.
- Aoyama, Hayakawa, Kinoshita & Nio computed the full **tenth-order (five-loop) QED** term, yielding **α⁻¹ ≈ 137.0359991736** (~0.25 ppb). Refs: Kinoshita review (RIKEN) · arXiv 2404.00649 · Wikipedia: Anomalous magnetic dipole moment

### Does α vary across space & time?
- **Webb–King–Murphy "dipole":** combining Keck + VLT quasar-absorption samples gives an angular dipole in α of amplitude **(0.97 +0.22/−0.20) × 10⁻⁵ at 4.1σ**, pointing toward RA ≈ 17.3h ± 1.0h, Dec ≈ −61° ± 10° — tentative evidence for spatial variation.
- **Whitmore & Murphy (2015)** re-analysis of modelling/systematic errors in quasar α measurements. MNRAS 454, 3082.
- **Planck intermediate results XXIV** — CMB constraints on varying fundamental constants. arXiv 1406.7482
- Latest lab/astro limits: sub-ppm ALMA limit, atomic-clock and highly-charged-ion tests (see Research by Theme below).

### Canonical reviews & references
- **Uzan, "Varying Constants, Gravitation and Cosmology,"** Living Reviews in Relativity 14, 2 (2011) — the standard review. arXiv 1009.5514
- **Uzan (2025)**, "Fundamental constants: from measurement to the universe" — updated review. arXiv 2410.07281
- **Bronnikov, Ivashchuk & Khruschov (2025)**, "The fine structure constant: a review of measurement results and possible space-time variations." arXiv 2506.18328
- Encyclopedic overview: Wikipedia, Fine-structure constant

### What it means
α ≈ **1/137.036** is the dimensionless coupling strength of the electromagnetic interaction — it sets how strongly light and matter interact, independent of any unit system. Because it is pure-number, it is the natural place to look for "varying constants" and connections to deeper structure (unification, extra dimensions, the golden ratio, quantum gravity). Feynman called ~137 *"one of the greatest damn mysteries of physics."*

### Historical & foundational references
- **Sommerfeld (1916)** — introduced α while extending Bohr's model to a relativistic theory of hydrogen; it appeared as the small dimensionless number governing the **fine-structure splitting** of spectral lines (hence the name).
- **Dirac's Large Numbers Hypothesis (1937)** — dimensionless combinations of constants might change over cosmic time — the intellectual seed of the "varying constants" program.
- **The Oklo natural reactor (Shlyakhter 1976; Damour & Dyson 1996)** — a ~1.8-billion-year-old natural fission reactor in Gabon. Because ¹⁴⁹Sm production hinges on a ≈0.1 eV neutron resonance, its isotope ratios bound α drift to roughly **−0.9×10⁻⁷ < Δα/α < 1.2×10⁻⁷ over 1.8 Gyr** — still one of the tightest geophysical limits. arXiv hep-ph/9606486 · arXiv 1510.00856
- **Webb, Flambaum, Murphy et al. (1999–2011)** — the "many-multiplet method" applied to quasar absorption spectra gave the first hints that α was **smaller in the distant past**, later developing into the tentative spatial dipole (Keck + VLT). Barrow & Webb "Varying Alpha": arXiv 0912.5510
- **Feynman on 137** — *"one of the greatest damn mysteries of physics: a magic number that comes to us with no understanding by man."*

## PAGE 3 — 📖 Review Summaries — Varying α
*Structured summaries of the two canonical reviews (generated from full texts, 9 Jul 2026)*

### Uzan (2011) — "Varying Constants, Gravitation and Cosmology"
Living Reviews in Relativity 14, 2 · arXiv 1009.5514

**Core thesis.** Fundamental "constants" may actually be dynamical fields that vary in space and time. Testing their constancy is a direct probe of general relativity, the equivalence principle, and physics beyond the Standard Model.

**Why it matters theoretically.** If a coupling constant varies in space, it necessarily induces a violation of the universality of free fall: composite masses depend on electromagnetic, strong, and weak binding energies, so composition-dependent variation produces anomalous accelerations. This ties constant-stability tests directly to tests of gravity.

**Observational constraints surveyed:** atomic clocks (present-day drift of α); the Oklo natural reactor (~1.8 Gyr ago); meteorite dating (decay rates sensitive to couplings); quasar absorption spectra (many-multiplet method); CMB & Big Bang Nucleosynthesis; stellar physics (evolution and white-dwarf cooling timescales).

**Theoretical frameworks.** Scalar-tensor gravity (varying G), string/Kaluza-Klein theories (varying constants from compactified extra dimensions), and unification schemes predicting correlated variation across couplings via renormalization-group relations.

**Takeaway.** No variation has been confirmed; the review frames constant-stability as a cornerstone test of local position invariance, with atomic clocks and quasar spectroscopy driving the tightest bounds.

### Bronnikov, Ivashchuk & Khruschov (2025) — "The Fine Structure Constant: A Review"
arXiv 2506.18328

**What it covers.** A 2025 synthesis of measurement techniques for α (central to the revised SI), recent experimental and theoretical results, and observational evidence for possible space-time variation.

**Laboratory methods.** Reviews the main determination methods for fundamental metrology, highlighting high-stability optical frequency standards — notably the ytterbium-171 ion — as the practical state of the art.

**Astrophysical / cosmological tests.** Surveys observational data on α variability across cosmic history from distant astronomical sources (quasar absorption and related probes of temporal/spatial change).

**Conclusions on variation.** Despite no experimentally confirmed long-term variation at high accuracy, the authors note persistent hints of slightly lower α in the remote past vs today, and flag unresolved inconsistencies among laboratory approaches.

**Takeaway.** No definitive variation is established, but real discrepancies in precision measurements and tantalizing cosmological hints remain; next-generation Yb-171 optical standards could settle whether α truly varies.

## PAGE 4 — 🔬 Fine Structure Digest — 2026-07-22 (latest daily digest)

A niche-topic run. Quanta Magazine's QED archive yielded three directly relevant pieces on the electromagnetic coupling, the running of α, and the divergence structure of QED perturbation theory. Scientific American's on-site search endpoint returned 404 this run and Popular Mechanics is blocked to the fetch agent, so both are noted without fresh items. α = e²/(4πε₀ℏc) ≈ 1/137.036.

**Quanta Magazine — "Analog vs. Digital: The Race Is On To Simulate Our Quantum Universe" (September 5, 2025).**
Physicists are now running genuine quantum simulations of QED itself — the theory whose coupling is α. A Muschik–Ringbauer team in Innsbruck used a qudit processor of five calcium-40 ions (five of eight electronic levels per quantum digit) to simulate a 2D patch of the electromagnetic field, observing virtual particle–antiparticle pairs appear and annihilate as the QED vacuum demands. Since the coupling sets the amplitude of these lattice pair-creation events, such simulators probe the strong-field, non-perturbative regime that fixes α. The stated frontier is QCD, whose running coupling α_s(Q²) grows at low energy (confinement), opposite to QED's α, which grows toward high energy.
quantamagazine.org/analog-vs-digital-the-race-is-on-to-simulate-our-quantum-universe-20250905/

**Quanta Magazine — "How to Tame the Endless Infinities Hiding in the Heart of Particle Physics" (April 6, 2023).**
QED's precision hides an uncomfortable fact: its perturbative expansion in powers of α is asymptotic and ultimately diverges. Writing an observable as A(α) = Σ cₙ αⁿ, the coefficients eventually grow factorially (cₙ ~ n!), giving zero radius of convergence — the series only approximates truth up to about n ~ 1/α ≈ 137 terms. 'Resurgence' reads the divergence pattern of the perturbative coefficients to reconstruct the missing non-perturbative physics, encoded in exponentially small instanton-like terms of order exp(−c/α) that no finite Feynman-diagram sum can see. For α this ambiguity is astronomically tiny, but the same math is decisive in strongly coupled theories — tying the smallness of α to why QED perturbation theory works so absurdly well.
quantamagazine.org/how-math-tames-the-infinities-of-quantum-field-theory-20230406/

**Quanta Magazine — "How Mathematical 'Hocus-Pocus' Saved Particle Physics" (September 17, 2020).**
This explainer on renormalization is the key to why α is not truly constant. Naive QED loop integrals diverge; renormalization absorbs the infinities into measured quantities and, as a byproduct, makes the coupling depend on the probing energy scale. The running coupling climbs from α(0) ≈ 1/137 at low energy to α(M_Z) ≈ 1/128 at the Z mass, because vacuum polarization screens the bare charge and the screening weakens at shorter distances. Formally the scale dependence obeys the RG equation μ² dα/dμ² = β(α) = α²/(3π) + …, with the positive QED beta function driving α upward with energy. The value of α is therefore a scale-dependent statement — a crucial caveat for anyone chasing a fundamental origin of 1/137.
quantamagazine.org/how-mathematical-hocus-pocus-saved-particle-physics-20200917/
