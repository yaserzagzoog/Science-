# Section 1 — Family Business Governance (Action Guide)

### Based on Dr Marleen Dieleman's OWP 2026 Session (IMD, Lausanne) — Applied to Al-Zagzoug

## 1.1 Diagnose Your Governance Gap First

Map yourself on two axes: **business complexity** vs **family complexity**.

**Business complexity drivers:** large revenue/assets, global footprint, multiple industries, sophisticated industries, public listing.

**Family complexity drivers:** larger number of owners, larger number of family members, multiple generations working together, global dispersion, prior conflicts.

**Al-Zagzoug reading:** Business complexity is clearly high — multi-channel retail (~100 stores), wholesale, projects, e-commerce, GlemGas agency, plus new ventures (BNPL, Atoco). The open question is family complexity. Score both honestly on the low/medium/high grid.

**Core rule of the session: the more complex the family and business, the more you need to love rules.**

## 1.2 Build Parallel Governance — Not Just Corporate Governance

Two mirrored structures, linked by two founding documents:

| Corporate Governance (business) | Bridge Documents | Family Governance (family) |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Shareholders | | Family Forum |
| Board | Articles of Association ↔ Family Constitution | Family Council |
| Management | | Family Office |

Pricing authority reforms, org restructuring, and systems work strengthen the **corporate** side. The **family** side needs equivalent formalization to close the governance gap.

## 1.3 Draft the Family Constitution (Six Sections)

1. **Intro / rationale / mission**
2. **Family values**
3. **Ownership** rules
4. **Family-business policies**
5. **Family decision-making structure**
6. **Amendments & signatures**

Adoption benchmark: Finland 63%, India 36%, Germany 19%, USA 18%, China 8%, Japan 4%. A written constitution is still a differentiator in most markets — and connects directly to the family constitution work already underway at Zagzoog.

## 1.4 Resolve the Two Structural Dilemmas Explicitly

**A. The Ownership Dilemma** — Allow fragmentation ↔ Consolidate ownership

Spectrum of options:

- No restrictions
- Transfer restrictions (limit to family)
- Organize representation (each voting block = one family branch)
- Active restrictions (link ownership to leadership)
- Place ownership in a trust or foundation

Decide deliberately — don't leave it to default inheritance.

**B. The Family-Business Dilemma** — Family first ↔ Business first

Spectrum of options:

- Everyone can join
- Close family-business connection
- **Combine family commitment with meritocracy** (the balanced middle)
- Only the best family members lead
- Pure meritocracy

Codify the choice in six family-business policies: **employment, remuneration, retirement, conflict, code of conduct, conflict of interest**.

## 1.5 Use the Fairness Framework to De-Escalate Disputes

Most family conflicts are clashes between *legitimate but different* justice principles:

| Principle | Core Idea | Tension |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Egalitarianism | Everyone morally equal → same resources | Ignores different contributions and needs |
| Luck egalitarianism | Inequality from luck is unjust; from choice is not | Hard to separate luck from choice |
| Difference principle | Inequality is just only if it benefits the least advantaged | Hard to identify the least advantaged |
| Utilitarianism | Best distribution = most good for most people | May sacrifice the minority |
| Desert (merit) | More for contribution, effort, or cost borne | Hard to define merit vs luck |
| Libertarian / Procedural | Fair, transparent, legally valid process | Fair processes can produce outcomes that feel unfair |

**Circle mapping:**

- Business circle → Desert, Utilitarian
- Family circle → Egalitarian, Difference principle, Utilitarian
- Ownership circle → Libertarian
- **Overlaps → reconciled through procedural justice (a constitution)**

**Practical three-step method:**

1. Recognize the fairness principle behind each person's position
2. Acknowledge its validity within its circle, and write clear policies
3. Negotiate overlaps via procedural justice — agreed rules, not case-by-case negotiation

## 1.6 Know Your Phase — and Prepare the Next One Early

**Controlling Founder → Sibling Partnership → Cousin Consortium → Business Family**

| Phase | Key traits | Governance need |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Controlling Founder | Owner-centric, fast decisions, key-person risk | Limited family governance |
| Sibling Partnership | Consensus decisions, conflict/paralysis risk | Some family governance to stabilize |
| Cousin Consortium | Slower group decisions, unequal involvement | Clear KPIs, fair process, formal family governance, outside expertise |
| Business Family | Joint decisions, most members not involved | Emotional connection investment, network-style organization |

Build the next phase's governance **before** the transition — not after conflict erupts.

## 1.7 Immediate Action Plan for Al-Zagzoug

1. **Score** family and business complexity on the grid (criteria in §1.1)
2. **Complete the transformation worksheet** — pick 2–3 actions per side:
   - *Business:* enhance shareholder voice · strengthen board(s) · enhance management quality · enforce systems and processes · accelerate talent development
   - *Family:* increase family unity · enhance family-business policies · set up/improve the family council · articulate family legacy & values · organize the family office
3. **Advance the family constitution** through the six-section structure (§1.3)
4. **Adopt the fairness vocabulary** in family discussions on remuneration, roles, and ownership (§1.5)
