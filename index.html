The mission

Coordination and throughput are the bottleneck — not ideas.

Collective AI brings students, researchers, faculty, and independent scientists together across regions to attack three open problems in the open. A frontier language model is not a replacement for scientists — it is the shared engine that accelerates literature synthesis, hypothesis triage, simulation code, cross-disciplinary translation, and the coordination of a distributed cohort. We formalize and stress-test claims; we don't assume them.

The organisation

A CERN for open, AI-native science

CERN gave particle physicists one shared instrument no single lab could build alone. Collective AI is the same idea for the AI era: a shared intelligence hub where researchers anywhere plug into frontier-scale AI capacity — long context, high throughput — to calibrate their thinking, pool what they learn, and build on one another's work.

Shared frontier-AI capacity

Every contributor works with a large-context, high-throughput model as their reasoning and analysis partner — no individual API bill or infrastructure to stand up.

Calibrate & align

A common analysis layer that checks derivations, reproduces results, and keeps everyone working from the same evidence base — so contributions compose instead of conflict.

Pool & broadcast knowledge

Findings, code, and prompts flow back into a shared corpus and weekly digests, so what one researcher learns immediately raises the whole cohort's floor.

Research programme

Three open problems in fundamental physics

Two mainstream open questions and one contested hypothesis we intend to formalize and falsify rather than advocate for. Each track links to a curated set of arXiv references and sources.

A

Quantum gravity & objective collapse

Penrose–Hameroff Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR)
τ ≈ ℏ / EG

Computationally formalize and critically evaluate gravitationally-induced wavefunction collapse. Derive collapse- and decoherence-time estimates for realistic microtubule parameters, identify falsifiable regimes, and document clearly where the model fails against known decoherence bounds.

⚠ Contested hypothesis — rigor, not advocacy References & sources on arXiv →
B

The fine-structure constant

Is the electromagnetic coupling constant across space and time?
α = e² / (4πε₀ℏc) ≈ 1 / 137.036

Synthesize quasar-absorption and laboratory-spectroscopy datasets, reproduce published variation analyses, and quantify systematic uncertainties — a recognized open question with concrete, data-driven tests.

References & sources on arXiv →
C

Dark matter

The missing-mass problem
flat v(r) ⇒ M(r) ∝ r · Ωdm ≈ 0.27

Comparative evaluation of candidate explanations — particle candidates (WIMP, axion) and modified-dynamics alternatives — against galactic rotation curves and gravitational-lensing data. Structured hypothesis triage: which models survive which datasets, and which observations would settle open cases.

Method

Five ways the AI hub accelerates the research

Concrete, measurable functions — the intelligence layer of a distributed research effort.

Literature synthesis & knowledge mapping

Long-context and Batch-API processing to map the state of each problem across thousands of papers — surfacing contradictions, dead ends, and gaps far faster than manual review.

Hypothesis generation & triage

Proposing testable predictions and ranking them by falsifiability and data availability, so effort goes to what is actually decidable.

Code & simulation

Writing and auditing Python analysis pipelines, reproducing published results, and running numerical estimates — collapse/decoherence times, α-variation fits, rotation-curve models.

Cross-disciplinary translation

Track A spans quantum gravity, physical chemistry, and neuroscience. The hub bridges notations, vocabularies, and languages so specialists can build on each other's work.

Coordination layer

Automated weekly "state-of-the-question" digests per track that keep 20–50 globally distributed contributors aligned — the collaboration function no institution currently owns.

Open, reproducible output

Every result ships with its code and prompts released publicly, so anyone can reproduce, critique, and extend the work.
